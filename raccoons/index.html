Pet door camera · 2020 to now

Night Shift at the Pet Door

A cat cam, a raccoon named Randy, ten RACCOON ALERT messages, barking dogs nobody asked for, and a nervous cat who now has her own announcement.

Three at once. One in the flap, one below, one off to the right.

Chapter 1 · 2019 to 2020

It started as a cat cam

We got Finn and Mara in May 2019. They came and went through a plain flap pet door that had gone in years before, for the dog we had back then. Indoor cats, outdoor cats, whatever they felt like that hour.

I just wanted to know when they left and when they came home. So in February 2020 I pointed a Raspberry Pi at the flap and started writing code. On March 12th the commit message reads "working version!!!", three exclamation marks and all. The server's web addresses still say /catcam/ to this day.

That spring I was scrolling through the night's movies and something that was very much not a cat walked up to the door. A raccoon. It never came in. But I started wondering how to build a door that closes for anything that isn't a cat.

Every design I sketched had the same problem. A door that slams shut when the wrong animal is in the flap is a door that can slam shut on the right one.

Every one of my pet door ideas looked like a cat guillotine.

And even if I'd built a door that didn't, there was a second problem: knowing when to close it. The camera could tell me something was at the flap. It couldn't tell me what. I tried several ways to teach it the difference between a cat and a raccoon, with less than limited success.

The raccoons didn't go away completely. Every so often one would turn up on the camera at night, but as far as we ever saw, none of them came in. So the idea went in a drawer, and the cat cam kept quietly running for five years.

The cast
Chapter 2 · March 2022

Percy moves in

I spent three months living in Amsterdam. When I got home, a cat I'd never seen was coming in through the flap like he paid rent. Tabby-ish, friendly, and a tail with rings on it, the kind of tail that makes you look twice in the dark. We called him Percy, and he stayed.

Chapter 3 · Early June 2025

Randy

Five years after the first raccoon, the old dog flap was still just a flap. One night my partner got up for a glass of water. There was Percy, walking away from the cat food. Nothing unusual about that. Percy loves his food.

Then she took another look. That was not Percy.

She headed off to get me, and on the way she saw him zoom across a clear stretch of floor and straight out the flap.

She woke me anyway. I jumped into jeans, boots, and a thick coat over a hoodie, because I had no idea what I was about to walk into. On the way, I grabbed a broom out of the closet. By the time I got out there, broom raised, the living room was empty.

She named him Randy.

Randy came back over the next few nights, and she'd spot him in the house. Her review: very respectable, never made a mess. As far as she was concerned we'd simply picked up another neighborhood cat, like Percy. Then Randy stopped coming, and she missed him.

When Randy finally did come back, he'd brought friends.

The friends were not respectable. The mornings started to come with questions. Did you move the cat food? Why are the games on the floor?

Eventually it occurred to us that we owned a camera pointed at the only way in. So we checked it. There they were: not just Randy, but the whole gang, coming in through the flap, several nights in a row, while we slept.

One respectable raccoon is a houseguest. A gang is a problem.

Chapter 4 · June to July 2025

A lot of CAT scans

About a week after the friends showed up, on June 19th, 2025, I gave up on ever building the guillotine and spent $181.39 on a SureFlap DualScan, a pet door that reads each cat's microchip and only unlocks for them. Plus $25 for expedited shipping, because raccoons. It arrived and was installed a couple of days later.

Then every cat had to be registered. You press a button on the door, then hold the cat in the doorway until it beeps. It's self-checkout at the grocery store, except the groceries have claws and you have to feed them through a small hole. Unexpected cat in bagging area.

I also hadn't figured out the settings. So I cleared the door and started over. Then I did it again. And again. Every reset meant catching all three cats and scanning them all over again. None of the scans were appreciated.

Mara appreciated hers least of all. Afterwards, every time the lock clicked, she ran. We were afraid she'd never come in again, so we switched the clever door off and left the flap open. No lock, no click. A $206 plain flap, expedited. Mara slowly forgave it.

The raccoons didn't need to forgive anything. They just needed to notice the flap was open again. It took them a few weeks, and this time the camera was saving every clip.

Two days later the camera started sending a picture to our phones every time something moved at the pet door. It had no idea what it was looking at. Cat, raccoon, a leaf: you got a picture. A lot of pictures.

Chapter 5 · Late July 2025

The dog defense

We went away for a trip, and my son and his wife house-sat with their dog, Milo. The raccoons came up to the door every night, took one sniff, and left. The camera has no raccoons inside from the week the dog was there. Best security system we ever had, and it ate kibble.

We came home in early August. So did the raccoons. Sometimes one, sometimes a crowd. The dog smell faded, and they started trying the flap again.

Chapter 6 · August 2025

Ten RACCOON ALERTs

This time I went for AI. Back in 2020, getting a computer to tell a cat from a raccoon was the part I couldn't crack. Five years later, I could just ask one. I bought a computer just for that job: an HP desktop with a graphics card big enough to run a vision AI at home, for about $1,000. It's called ada. On August 9th I started a little project that sends the camera's movie to a vision model running on ada and asks what it sees. On August 14th the camera asked its first question.

Once it could tell a raccoon from a cat, the alerts got serious. One message for the picture. Then ten more, in capitals, to make sure somebody actually woke up.

cmo alerts04:40

It worked, in the sense that somebody woke up. By the time I'd made it down the hall to the living room, where the cat food lives, the raccoons had already left.

Chapter 7 · Summer 2026

Raccoon summer

The raccoons came back in May 2026 and really settled in at the end of July. On July 30th and 31st the camera caught 26 raccoon clips in two nights, often two or three at once, pawing at the open flap.

By then I had a routine. The Google Home would sound a raccoon alert, I'd go out with a flashlight, and the raccoons would leave. Not far, though. I could hear them on the other side of the fence, waiting for me to go back to bed.

On July 31st, at 9:47 pm, in the middle of the evening's visit, I decided to settle it. Jeans, boots, thick coat, broom: the full outfit from the night I went looking for Randy. Only this time it was Randy and his gang. I opened the gate and stepped through to chase them off, I suppose. It was not a well-thought-out plan.

On the other side of the gate I heard them rustling in the bushes. Then nothing at all. Just me in the dark, in a winter coat in July, holding a broom.

I went back in. The Google Home went off again just before midnight, and I got up again. And again at 4:32, about an hour before the sun came up. Every time, they'd gone by the time I got there.

After that night, I kept the broom next to the bed.

Raccoons: 26 clips in two nights. Me: one broom, zero raccoons.

Meanwhile the AI had a confession to make. Between May and July, the model I was using called my cats "raccoon" about ninety times, and its favourite suspect was Percy. Remember the tail? A striped cat with a ringed tail, at night, at the flap where the raccoons stand. The early versions saw that tail and shouted raccoon, over and over. Percy did not appreciate being called a raccoon.

Try it yourself. Which one is the raccoon?

So I started testing. I hand-labeled clips, scored every change to the model and the prompt against them, and only kept what scored better. Bit by bit Percy stopped being a raccoon. These days the AI almost always calls him what he is: a cat with a very misleading tail. Percy feels much better about it.

Chapter 8 · August 2026

Who let the dogs out

The afternoon after the broom night, I tried something else. Another trip was coming up, and Milo wouldn't be house-sitting this time. If a real dog worked, maybe a fake one would. So when the AI saw a raccoon, the Google Home in the living room started barking.

Volume up. You'll see why the cats objected.

Because of how the camera works, the barking came a bit late: it had to finish the movie, then ask the AI, then tell the speaker. I'm fairly sure some nights the raccoons ate, left, and were halfway down the street before the dogs went off.

The cats, on the other hand, heard every single bark. They hated it. The raccoons, as far as I can tell, did not care at all.

Chapter 9 · September 2026

We lock the door

Around September 16th we turned the microchip lock back on. The camera tells the rest of the story: the day before, 25 trips through the door. The next two days, four and three. Then the cats started sitting outside it, staring at it, waiting for a human. Some days there were a hundred events of a cat looking at a door.

Finn and Percy figured it out within a couple of weeks. Mara didn't. The raccoons kept coming all through September, pawing at a flap that no longer opened, and went home with nothing. The last one so far showed up on October 15th at 4:40 am.

Chapter 10 · October 2026

There's a cat at the door

So the raccoon project turned into a cat butler. Now when the camera sees a cat waiting, the speakers in the house say so, and whoever hears it gets up and opens the door.

The same Google voice the speakers use.

Mara will now use the pet door to go out. She will not use it to come back in. She sits on the deck, the house announces her, and someone lets her in like a hotel guest.

Somewhere in there a porch camera joined the system too: a store-bought one that pinged my phone every time the wind blew, until it got rewired into my own setup. That's a story for another night.

Six years of code, an AI on my own computer looking at 900 clips a day, and the main thing it does is make sure I let the cat in.

Next I'm setting up a new Pi camera, this time in a way anyone could do, and maybe letting other people use my server. The code is on GitHub.

If you want to know how it works (the computer, the AI, how I test it and what it costs), I wrote that up too: How the pet door camera works.
